Warm UI v2 · coffee-joke resolution · light + dark · SOCDESK

Keep the coffee brown. Put the vibrant on the interface.

The mug is the joke — SOC analysts run on coffee — so the liquid must read as coffee, hardest of all at 16–24px. Setting the liquid to the vibrant accent turned it into a milkshake and the gag fell flat. Resolution: the mark's liquid is a real mid-roast brown; the vibrant accent moves onto the product (active tab, buttons, links, focus, the DESK accent, data highlights); and on the emblem the accent survives as the rising steam — energy above, coffee below. Four zoned roles, no collisions: warm neutrals = the room, coffee = the mark, vibrant = the product, green/amber/red = verdict. Shown in full light and dark, and pressure-tested on the one surface that must stay sober — the escalation card.

01 · The coffee joke lands

Before / after: pink liquid vs coffee liquid

Same pixel mug, same grid — only the liquid changes. The accent-liquid version stops reading as coffee; the brown-liquid version is unmistakable, and the accent still rides the mark as steam.

Before · accent liquid

Reads as a milkshake. Vibrant liquid fights the gag — a tired analyst's cup isn't bubblegum. At 16px it's just "a mug of something."

After · coffee liquid + accent steam

Unmistakably coffee. Mid-roast body, a golden crema line, warm rim — the joke lands. The accent lives in the steam, so the mark still carries brand energy.

02 · The vibrant accent

Periwinkle vs raspberry — and the one that fights the verdict

Both are off-kilter and non-cyber. The deciding factor is the tally: the accent must never sit in the same colour family as a verdict tone, or "red = bad" gets muddy. Amethyst is out (purple is the AI-tool tell). Each shown with its Copy button beside the green/amber/red verdict dots, in both grounds.

Periwinkle#7C8AFF

Adjacency risk. Raspberry #F0509A is one hue-step from the red verdict #F5566B — on the tally the accent and the "malicious" dots share a pink-red family. Boldest, most ownable, warmest; but it taxes the verdict read.

04 · Four colours, four jobs

Is that one colour too many? No — because every role is zoned

The system stays clean only if each colour has one job and one place. Coffee-brown is mark-only (it never becomes a UI control, so it can't be mistaken for interactive or semantic). The vibrant is UI-only. Verdict tones are tally-only. Break those walls and it turns to mud; keep them and four colours read as a language.

Warm neutralsThe room. Espresso / paper base, cream / ink text, caramel structure.
Coffee brownThe mark's joke. Mid-roast + crema. Lives only inside the mug — never in UI.
Vibrant accentThe product's energy. Tabs, buttons, links, focus, DESK, data — never a verdict.
Green · amber · redVerdict meaning. The tally only. Reserved so a flag always reads as a flag.
My read. The warm + vibrant direction holds as a system — but only with the role-split enforced. Keep the mug's liquid a real mid-roast #A6612F (with a crema highlight and warm rim) so the coffee gag lands at every size, including 16px where the steam disappears and the brown band does the work — no saucer needed. Move the vibrant entirely onto the interface, and let it ride the emblem only as the steam. For the accent I recommend Periwinkle (#7C8AFF dark / #4A4FD0 light): it's the only candidate with zero adjacency to the red verdict tone, it gives blue's actionable affordance, and the warm/cool tension against espresso reads designed rather than loud. Raspberry is the bolder, warmer alternate, but #F0509A sits one hue-step from the red verdict — it taxes the one read the product can't afford to lose. And the escalation card proves the thesis: witty identity, professional work product — the playfulness lives in the mark and chrome; the artifact that travels into a ticket stays sober, tally-led, and client-safe.
