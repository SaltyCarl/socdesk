Brand implementation · hybrid translation · SOCDESK

The bar-chart mug, de-slopped into our warm-espresso / periwinkle system.

The owner's reference renders this mark as a glossy gold mug with electric-blue glossy bars and a heavy neon glow on a black void. That skin is rejected. This is the hybrid: the mug body becomes our warm coffee-brown (#A6612F, mark-only — never gold, which is reserved for verdicts), the bars become periwinkle (#7C8AFF dark / #4A4FD0 light — the product/data colour), and the ground is warm-espresso (dark) or warm-paper (light) — never black. Everything is flat, crisp, scalable SVG: no glow, no bloom, no gloss, no gradient-as-decoration. Both themes below sit side-by-side; fonts are self-hosted; zero external requests.

00 · The mark

The primary mark — coffee vessel, periwinkle signal rising inside

A coffee mug (open rim, D-handle) drawn in our warm coffee-brown, holding three ascending periwinkle bars — the "signal rising / intelligence" motif. The role split does the storytelling for free: the vessel is coffee (the mark tone), the data is periwinkle (the product tone). Two brand tones, nothing else. The coffee is theme-fixed; only the periwinkle retunes between themes.

    08 · Honest read & tweaks
    
Does flat periwinkle bars-in-a-mug actually work? Yes — with two moves

    
The concept reads: a coffee vessel holding rising periwinkle data is a genuinely distinctive CTI mark (nobody else owns
      "instrument that measures your coffee-break"), and it survives 16px once reduced. Two recommendations.

  

  

    
Bar count: three big, two smallrecommended
      
Three ascending bars is the minimum that reads as a chart (two reads as a step, four is dead weight even at
        32px). So: three bars for the detailed mark, and drop to two for the 16/24/32 favicon. Four bars — as in
        the reference — is rejected on legibility grounds at every size.

    
Let the tall bar break the rim at favicon sizerecommended
      
Inside the cup, an ascending chart is legible but the "rising" cue is quiet small. Pushing the tall bar just
        above the rim gives an unmistakable vertical signal at 16px — and it quietly inherits the shipped mark's
        "signal rising out of the cup" equity (the old steam) while staying a bar, not a wisp. This is the "rise" cut.

    
Steam vs bars — keep bars, borrow the risedecision
      
A steam wisp is marginally cleaner at 16px, but it makes the mark a generic "coffee + steam" icon and throws away
        the one idea that differentiates it — the chart. The "rise" cut keeps the bar-chart concept and gets
        most of steam's small-size clarity. Recommend bars; do not replace them with steam.
