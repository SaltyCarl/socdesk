BRANDING & IDENTITY · ROUND 2 · SOCDESK

The mark, expanded — steam restored, the mug made the "O", and the badge you asked for

Round 1 cut the steam and settled the wordmark and typeface. This round answers the four notes left on that sheet, drawing straight from the pixel coffee-mug heritage mark on SanchezOnSecurity.com without copying it wholesale. Palette, wordmark casing (fused-caps SOCDESK) and typeface (Red Hat Display + Red Hat Mono) are settled and untouched — this sheet is only about the mark and the lockup. Every candidate is judged three ways: at 96px, at 24px (the favicon test), and dropped into the real product topbar. A mark that only survives large is disqualified.

  • ↳ steam back? Lane A keeps it as heritage pixel dithering; Lane B restores it onto the monoline mug in three disciplined treatments.
  • ↳ vertical SOC / DESK? Lane D — stacked wordmark, mark above or mark-as-the-O in the top line.
  • ↳ SD(mug) + SOCDESK under? Lane E is the literal read; Lane C is the stronger "or something similar" — the mug becomes the O in SOC.

Emblem / app-icon: Lane C — mug-as-O (S·mug·C over DESK) · Topbar mark: Lane B1 — monoline mug, single steam wisp · Favicon: Lane A2 — pixel top-down mug · Full reasoning & assembled preview at the end.

Lane C · The emblem — mug-as-O (HERO)

S · mug · C = "SOC", with DESK beneath

This is the direct SOCDESK translation of the owner's own site mark, where the mug is already the "O" in S-O-S. "SOC" also has an O — so the mug becomes the O in SOC, and DESK sets beneath it as a stamped sub-line. It answers "SD(mug) with SOCDESK underneath" more strongly than a literal SD would: the mark now spells the product instead of decorating two initials. The logo-intelligence read backs this — a lettermark built on "interlocking letters and negative space" is the recommended route for a name like this. Shown as a pixel cut (heritage) and a monoline cut (clean).

Recommendation & assembled system

One coherent family — you pick, this is the case

These are real options and the call is the owner's. But if the goal is one identity that spans an app icon, a topbar and a 16px favicon while protecting the coffee-mug heritage, the pieces below fit together with no new asset pipeline — every mark is the same three-stroke mug topology already in the live SVG, plus pixel grids that are direct descendants of the existing favicon.

Why this trio

  1. Emblem / app-icon → Lane C2 (mug-as-O). It is the only candidate where the mark and the name are the same object; it is the direct SOCDESK sibling of the owner's existing S-O-S mug mark, and it answers "SD(mug) + SOCDESK" more strongly than SD itself.
  2. Topbar mark → Lane B1 (single-wisp mug). Restores the steam the owner missed, drops into the current topbar as one extra path, and holds together at 24px where twin curls fall apart.
  3. Favicon → Lane A2 (pixel top-down mug). A literal descendant of the site favicon, crisp to 16px, and the same circle that becomes the O in the emblem — so the smallest and the largest marks rhyme.

Sleeper: Lane B3, the rising tri-dot. If the steam becomes a live connected / on-watch status pip, the mark stops being decorative and earns a functional job in the UI — the most "product-native" idea here, even if it reads less literally as steam.
