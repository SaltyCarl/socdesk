Palette exploration · SOCDESK · internal review

Keep warm & periwinkle, or re-skin the room?

Three palette directions, each judged on the same real UI — the brand lockup, the live topbar, and a Core-radar verdict card — in both themes, so the decision rests on how the scheme behaves in product, not on swatches. The brand mark is locked (execution B, Shaded Dimension): a dimensional caramel mug with one ceramic shadow facet, a coffee crescent, and pixel steam — flat, no glow. What's on the table is the room and the accent around it.

01

Warm-everywhere + periwinkle CURRENT · BASELINE

The control. Warm-espresso dark / warm-paper light, periwinkle accent, verdict tones held apart. Everything is one temperature: the mug doesn't have to fight the room because the room is already warm. The question every alternative has to beat: does a change actually serve the analyst, or just look different?

02

Graphite ground + periwinkle WARMTH RESERVED TO THE BRAND

A cooler neutral console: low-saturation graphite (a whisper of blue, deliberately not teal), periwinkle accent unchanged, and the caramel confined to the mug only. The bet is simultaneous contrast — a lone warm object pops harder against a cool ground, so the mug becomes the signature instead of blending into a warm room. The risk is losing the distinctive warmth that keeps SOCDesk from reading like generic console SaaS.

Genuinely different

03

Warm ground + gold/amber accent MONOCHROMATIC

Drop periwinkle for a warm gold accent — one temperature, maximum cohesion, and the steam turns gold so the whole mark is a single warm family. The honest problem, shown not buried: the accent gold lands in the exact hue family as the reserved verdict-amber. Look at each specimen's active nav tab and toggle pill against the amber chip in the card footer — on paper the accent and the "suspicious" verdict tone are nearly the same colour. That's a semantic collision (is this gold thing a control, or a severity signal?), and it breaks the reserved-tone rule.

Keep the current warm / periwinkle scheme — #2 differs, #3 fails.

The mug is the fixed point: a coffee mug forces warmth into the identity no matter what, so the only real choice is where that warmth lives. The current scheme lets it live everywhere and stays coherent; periwinkle earns its keep precisely because it is the one hue that is neither warm-neutral nor a verdict tone, so it can carry tabs, links, focus and data highlights without ever being mistaken for severity. That is a real functional property for a SOC tool, not a taste call.

Direction #2 (graphite + periwinkle) is the only alternative that respects all three constraints, and its simultaneous-contrast argument is real — the caramel mug genuinely reads more like a signature on the cool ground. But it wins on novelty, not on the analyst's job: it swaps a distinctive warm identity for a cooler neutral that reads like a dozen other consoles, and it risks drifting toward the very cyber-console look the brand set out to avoid. Worth prototyping only if the owner specifically wants to cool the room; it is a lateral move, not an upgrade.

Direction #3 (gold monochrome) is the clearest finding: it collides with the reserved verdict-amber, and the collision is worst exactly where analysts read severity — on the light theme the gold accent (#8A5E0A) and the amber verdict tone (#B4740C) are all but the same colour, so an active tab and a "suspicious" flag stop being distinguishable by hue. It also costs the mug its cool counter-note (gold steam sits almost on the crema), thinning the tonal separation that makes it read as coffee. Cohesive to look at, but it breaks the one rule the product can't break. Recommendation: keep #1; if a re-skin is truly wanted, prototype #2; do not pursue #3.
