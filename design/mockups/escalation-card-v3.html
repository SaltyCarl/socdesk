Escalation card v3 · Copy text + Copy card · hero geolocation · SOCDESK

One card, two ways out — portable text, and a picture that shows where it's from.

Plain text can't carry a graphic, and rich-HTML copy gets stripped unpredictably by email and ticket sanitizers — so the one reliable way to drop a visual into a pasted email is to copy an image (PNG). That also automates the product's north-star step: screenshot the reputation, paste into the email. So the escalation card gains a second copy action. Copy text emits the attributed plain-text block (unchanged from palette-warm-v2) for plain-text ticket systems; Copy card renders the card to a clean PNG for client-facing email and rich targets — and the PNG leads with the geolocation: an embedded, self-drawn dot-matrix world map with a pin at ipinfo's lat/long. Same honesty policy, richer format. Shown in full light and dark.

01 · Copy text / Copy card

The escalation card gets a second button

The on-screen card stays the sober work product it was: tally-led, attributed, restrained. What changes is the action row — two encodings of the same honest content. Copy card is the primary action (it's the client-facing artifact and the north-star step); Copy text stays for plain-text ticket queues. Neither button carries a recommendation, and both are labelled by their target, not by a verdict.

PRODUCTION — the mock above is HTML/SVG so the design reads in the browser, but production renders the card on <canvas> and writes it with navigator.clipboard.write([ new ClipboardItem({ 'image/png': blob }) ]). No external dependencies, no fonts or tiles fetched at capture time — CSP default-src 'none' safe. ipinfo already returns country / city / ASN / lat-long; the locator is drawn from those numbers, entirely client-side, and the coordinates never leave the browser. The embedded world landmask is a fixed, public-domain-style outline that ships with the page — the same bytes every time, leaking nothing about the target.
03 · Same policy, richer format

What the picture is still not allowed to say

A prettier artifact must not become a looser one. Every VERDICT-LANGUAGE guarantee that governs the plain-text block governs the PNG, verbatim.

The tally leads, as a count

The first content block is "5 of 6 public sources flagged this as adverse" — a fact, not an opinion. No SOCDesk-voice verdict word (malicious / suspicious / safe / clean) appears anywhere on the card.

Every source is attributed

Each row names the source and states its raw finding as fact (counts, ratios, catalog verbs). The gauge shows one segment per source, coloured by that source's own verdict — the count is visual, still attributed.

Geo is context, never the verdict

The hero is labelled "context — not a verdict." Geolocation is drawn in periwinkle (the product/data colour), never a verdict tone, and it never enters the N-of-M tally — exactly as VERDICT-LANGUAGE §1 requires of ipinfo.

Coverage & dual-use are honest

The Tor-exit dual-use caveat is on the card, and "Not consulted: urlscan" keeps the denominator honest — 5 of 6 consulted, not a hidden 5 of 7.

The verify caveat travels with it

"Not independently verified; verify before any blocking or client-facing action" is baked into the image, so it can't be lost when the picture is forwarded.

No recommendation, decision stays with the analyst

Per §4, the card carries no "escalate / block / monitor." What to do is the analyst's words in the ticket. The buttons are labelled by target — Copy card / Copy text — not by an action.

My read. Two artifacts, one card. The plain-text Copy text block is untouched, so plain-text queues still get the portable, sanitizer-proof version. Copy card solves the real problem — a graphic can't survive a plain-text field and rich-HTML paste is unreliable, so the only dependable visual is a PNG on the clipboard, which also automates the north-star "screenshot the reputation, paste into email" step. Making geolocation the hero is the right call for a client-facing image: it's the one piece of context a client grasps instantly, and rendering it as SOCDESK's own dot-matrix world map (pixel identity) with a pin drawn from ipinfo's lat/long keeps it CSP-safe and leak-free — no tile host ever learns what you're looking at. The honesty policy is preserved exactly: tally-led count, attributed sources, geo marked context, verify caveat, no recommendation, no verdict word. It reads urgent, survives a client reading it, and leaves the decision to the analyst. One concern to flag: the flag chip uses the national colours (which include a red and a gold band near — but clearly distinct in shape from — the verdict tones); I kept it small and unmistakably flag-shaped, and recommend the drawn 3-band chip over an emoji flag glyph because emoji flags don't render on canvas on Windows (they fall back to "DE" letters). The country name is always spelled out so nothing depends on the flag rendering.
